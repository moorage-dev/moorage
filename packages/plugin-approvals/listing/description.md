A second person OKs a page before it goes live. Writers keep pressing Publish; when a page needs approval, the publish is held and becomes a request. No account and no key.

## What it does

- **Publish asks for approval.** On the content types you choose, Publish and Schedule are held and the writer reads "Sent for approval. Not live yet." Contributors get an **Ask for approval** button.
- **Approve and publish in one step**, approve to publish later, or **send it back** with a note.
- **What changed, in words**: `Content: "£3.20" becomes "£3.40"`, compared with the live page, and after an approval with the approved version.
- **A text copy of each approved version**, kept in the history, so nobody can say they never saw it.
- **Approvers can fix a typo**: edit, save, approve. The history says it was edited by the approver.
- **Two plain choices**: "Others need my OK before publishing" or "I need someone else's OK before publishing". Nobody approves their own request.
- **AI tools connected to your site always need a person's OK**, on every content type.
- **An Approvals page and a dashboard widget**: what is waiting, what was sent back, every decision.
- **Email**, when your site can send it: to approvers when someone asks, to the writer when they decide, one reminder after two days.

## What it does not do

- It cannot hold what EmDash saves straight to a live page: the SEO panel, categories, tags and bylines of a page already live, and every save in a content type without drafts. The Settings tab lists them.
- No discussion threads, chains of approvers or approval by several people.

## Permissions

Holds publishing (content policy); reads content, drafts and the content schema to show what changed; publishes when an approver presses Approve and publish; reads users' names and emails to address approvers; sends email.
